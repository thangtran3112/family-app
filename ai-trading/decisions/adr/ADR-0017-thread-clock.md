# ADR-0017: A day-trade thread has one clock, and its controls sit above every name

Status: accepted 2026-11-01, owner's directive (built on `feature/thread-clock-fix`).

Decision: the time of a day-trade thread is ONE clock for every ticker on its list. Play, Pause, Step, speed, Jump and the time readout belong to the thread. They never sit under, inside or beside one name's chart, and nothing at the name level fast-forwards.

- **The clock was already shared; the screen did not say so.** `day_lists.revealed_through` is the thread's clock (`replay/session.py`: `Ctx.day_clock`, written by `advance()` as the larger of itself and the target). Each name keeps its own session row, whose `revealed_through` is the part of that name's chart revealed so far. Another name's session is lifted to the clock the next time it is opened or advanced (`start_session`; a swing session on load; `advance()` adds the away span), so no session is written while it is not on screen. A name left behind shows its Catch up banner and cannot be marked until it is lifted. This was designed in 02o-b2 and is unchanged.
- **Placement.** `ThreadClockBar` is the first thing in the replay workspace under the Back link, across the whole width, above the name's header: the label "Thread time" (a session that stands alone says "Replay time"), the clock, the player state, the status line, Play, Step, 1x 2x 5x 10x, Jump and the timeline. Its menu opens downward. It stays in full screen. The ticker header (`ReplayHeader`) carries the name's price, VWAP, prior close and P/L only: no clock, no player state. `ChartToolbar`, under the chart and labelled "<SYMBOL> chart", carries what belongs to one name: the candle interval (or the swing Daily toggle), Buy, Sell and Close position (1180), Chart settings, Full screen and the key legend.
- **The bar shows the thread's time, not a name's playhead.** `threadClock(revealedThrough, dayClock)` (`lib/replay/clock.ts`) is the later of the two. A name behind the clock shows the thread's time; Play ticks and Jump start from it.
- **Step is a step of the thread's clock.** `advance(step="bar" | "next_signal")` measures from `max(previous, day_clock)`, so from a name behind the clock it goes one bar (or one signal) past the clock instead of catching up and leaving the thread's time where it was. The catch-up, the away span, the stop pause (first stop crossed on any open name) and the no-lookahead cut are unchanged. An advance with an explicit `to` is unchanged.
- **Not done: eager advance of every session.** Writing every name's session whenever the clock moves would fill working orders and score signals on names she is not watching. Lifting on open keeps those rules in one place (`advance()` covers `previous → target` when the name is lifted, so fills and stop crossings are still found).
- **Tests.** `tests/replay/test_replay_switch.py` (step from a name behind the clock; next signal; a standalone session), `lib/replay/clock.test.ts` (`threadClock`), and two e2e tests in `e2e/desk/practice-switch.spec.ts` (layout at 1440 and 1180; a name left behind shows the thread's time and steps from it).
